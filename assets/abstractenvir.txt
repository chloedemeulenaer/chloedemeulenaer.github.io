When policies rely on individual choice, who reaps the gains? We study how choice environments shape the quality of health insurance choices in the Netherlands. Individuals choose a deductible annually: many pick a low deductible, despite substantial potential savings from a higher one. We establish that workplaces, neighbourhoods and insurance providers causally affect these choices. The effects are substantial even in contexts that do not directly involve insurance decisions: moving to a firm or neighbourhood where high-deductible take-up is 10 percentage points higher increases individual take-up by approximately 1.5 percentage points. Across all three environments, effects are concentrated among predictably healthy individuals, who end up making better decisions as a result. More educated individuals both encounter better environments and respond more strongly to them, contributing to the education gap in choice quality. Our simulations attribute 39% to 78% of the education gap in choice quality to these environmental effects, driven primarily by differences in responsiveness.
