This project uses new survey data from France to investigate the relative unpopularity of the inheritance tax. The tax is perceived as a tax on hard working individuals rather than on the wealthy: eliciting the perceived fairness of the inheritance tax, or of a tax “on the inheritance of hard working parents” yields similar results. Self-interest and knowledge of the tax explain little variation in support. Instead, equity and efficiency concerns, and most of all, beliefs about the effect of taxes on equity and efficiency, play a much larger role. Support for the inheritance tax is not immutable: a quote highlighting the unequal distribution of inherited wealth significantly increases support; while a quote stating that the tax is an unfair tax on a lifetime of hard work backfires on the (minority) of individuals who do not fully agree with it, increasing their support for the inheritance tax. Those asymmetrical effects suggest that challenging dominant narratives can be effective to shift opinions, even on a topic as polarised as the inheritance tax.
