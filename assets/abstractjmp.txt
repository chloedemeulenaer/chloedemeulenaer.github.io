When people disagree on economic policy, is it because they differ in their values, or in their beliefs about what those policies do? Using a large survey of over 7,000 French respondents, I measure beliefs about the impact of 29 economic reforms, along with a rich array of attitudes, preferences, and demographics. Participants favour reforms that they believe benefit them, and minimum wage workers; the strongest predictor of support, however, is the reforms perceived impact on economic growth. Policy impact beliefs explain 62% of the explained variation in support for economic policies, more than a set of attitudes that the literature has found to be robust predictors of support for redistribution. Differences in policy impact beliefs represent 44% of the explained partisan gap, and 93% of the explained preference for predistributive over redistributive reforms. Participants beliefs are more pessimistic than economists; providing research-based information does not significantly correct those misperceptions, but does increase support for progressive reforms. This project identifies policy impact beliefs as a key, underexplored determinant of public support for economic policies.
