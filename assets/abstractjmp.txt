When people disagree on economic policy, is it because they differ in their values, or in their beliefs about what those policies do? Using a large survey of over 7,000 French respondents, I measure beliefs about the effect of 29 economic reforms in a unified, comparable way, along with a rich array of attitudes, preferences, and demographics. Participants favour reforms that they believe benefit them, and minimum wage workers; the strongest predictor of support, however, is the reform’s perceived impact on economic growth. Policy impact beliefs explain 62% of the explained variation in support for economic policies, more than a set of attitudes established as robust predictors of support for redistribution. Differences in policy impact beliefs capture 46% of the explained partisan gap, and 93% of the explained preference for predistributive over redistributive reforms. Participants perceive the negative effects of contractionary reforms more strongly than the positive effects of expansionary ones, an asymmetry reminiscent of loss aversion. Providing research-based information does not significantly correct misperceptions, but does increase support for progressive reforms. This project identifies policy impact beliefs as a key, underexplored determinant of public support for economic policies.
