When people disagree on economic policy, is it because they differ in their values, or in what they think those policies do? Using a large survey of over 7,000 French respondents, I measure support for 29 economic reforms along with beliefs about their incidence, their impact on the budget and on the macroeconomy (“policy impact beliefs”). Participants favour reforms that they believe benefit them, and minimum wage workers; however the strongest predictor of support is the reform’s perceived impact on economic growth. Policy impact beliefs explain more variation in support than a range of variables shown to robustly predict support for redistribution, such as ideology or zero-sum thinking (“attitudes”). Half of the explained partisan gap is due to differences in policy impact beliefs—left- and right-wing participants have a similar mapping of beliefs to support, except for the greater weight put by left-wing individuals on the wellbeing of welfare recipients. Differences in policy impact beliefs also explain 94% of the explained preference for predistributive over redistributive reforms. Compared to the economic literature, participants underestimate the positive effects of expansionary reforms, and perceive policies as more regressive on average. Providing research-based information does not significantly correct misperceptions, but does increase support for progressive reforms. This project identifies policy impact beliefs as a key, underexplored determinant of public support for economic policies.
